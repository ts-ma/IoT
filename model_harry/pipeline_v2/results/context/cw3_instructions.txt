Task Description
This coursework involves the development, demonstration, and final written report (max. 4,000
words) for the activity recognition system.

Implementation
Your task will be to implement an activity recognition system for a set of activities listed below,
by applying machine learning techniques on RESpeck accelerometer data and displaying
real-time results in a mobile application.

You will experience the different stages in the design and implementation of a complex system,
from its specification to the demonstration of a working prototype and evaluation of its
performance. You will be exposed to aspects of sensor data analytics using machine learning
techniques, mobile application development, user interface design, and system integration and
testing.

There will be an opportunity to demonstrate progress and receive written formative feedback in
Week 6. The final presentation to showcase your prototype with a live demonstration is
scheduled on 18 November 2026 (10:00-13:00). Your final written report will be due on 15
January 2027.

Tasks
There are three main tasks to be completed for Coursework 3. They are outlined as follows.

2. Social Signals Classification

You should implement a model or set of models that is able to classify the following classes:

    2.​ Coughing (all stationary activities performed while coughing should be combined into

3. Mobile App Implementation

You should integrate the methods developed in Tasks 1-2 into your mobile application. Your app
should be able to classify physical activities and social signals in real-time. Your app should also
be able to keep a history of the different activities and social signals performed each day. For
example, users should be able to see that on October 17, they spent 1 hour walking, 10 hours
sitting/standing, coughed 30 times, etc.

Project Technical Evaluation [55/95 marks]

In terms of the technical evaluation of your project, you will be marked based on three
categories of the project: (1) Physical Activity Classification, (2) Social Signals Classification,
and (3) Mobile App Implementation.


1. Daily Physical Activity Classification [15/55 marks]

Accuracy will be evaluated using subject-wise 10-fold cross-validation, using the fold
assignments provided on Learn. Because not every subject recorded every activity, and subject
IDs are not shared across activities, folds are defined separately for each activity: each activity's
subjects are divided into 10 roughly equal groups, and in each fold, one group from every
activity is held out for testing while the model is trained on the rest. Within each activity, a
subject's data must never appear in both the training and test sets of the same fold.

Accuracy is calculated for each class separately and then averaged across the classes in each
group, so that activities with more subjects do not carry more weight. The reported figure is the
mean of this value across all 10 folds.
   ●​ Static activities (6 marks): Achieve a mean classification accuracy of at least 90%
       across the following five classes: sitting or standing (treated as a single class), lying on
       your left side, lying on your right side, lying on your back, and lying on your stomach.
   ●​ Dynamic activities (9 marks): Achieve a mean classification accuracy of at least 85%
       across the following six classes: walking, ascending stairs, descending stairs, running or
       jogging, shuffle walking, and miscellaneous movements.

2. Social Signals Classification [20/55 marks]

Accuracy will be evaluated using the same subject-wise 10-fold cross-validation and fold
assignments described in Section 1. For each class below, accuracy is the proportion of that
class's test samples correctly classified, and the reported figure is the mean of this value across
all 10 folds.

    ●​   Normal breathing (5 marks): Achieve a mean classification accuracy of at least 75%.
    ●​   Coughing (5 marks): Achieve a mean classification accuracy of at least 75%.
    ●​   Hyperventilation (5 marks): Achieve a mean classification accuracy of at least 75%.
    ●​   Other (5 marks): Achieve a mean classification accuracy of at least 75% for the "other"
         class, which includes (e.g. talking, eating, singing, laughing), treated as a single class.

* Note - Not achieving the specified accuracy threshold does not imply that you get zero marks.
You will still get rewarded marks should your accuracy be lower than the threshold.
